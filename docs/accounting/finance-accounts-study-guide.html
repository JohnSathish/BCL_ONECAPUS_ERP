Chapter 1

Why college accounting exists

A college is not only an academic institution. It is also a financial organisation. Every day money moves: students pay fees, the college pays salaries, electricity bills, vendors, scholarships, and bank deposits. Management, auditors, the university, and funding agencies all ask the same question in different words:

“Where did the money come from, where did it go, and what is left?”

The Finance & Accounts module answers that question in a structured, auditable way. Fees module tells you which student paid. Accounts tells you how that payment sits in the college books.

1.1 Two systems that must stay friends

ModuleJobExample question it answers
Fees / Student Finance Student-wise dues, receipts, concessions Does BA25-814 still owe exam fee?
Finance & Accounts Institution-wide books of account How much tuition income did we earn in June?
Payroll Staff salary calculation & payment What is net pay for employee EMP-102?
Accounts (again) Posts salary into ledgers What is total Salaries expense this month?

1.2 What “books of account” means

Think of the college as keeping a set of notebooks (today: digital ledgers). Each notebook tracks one kind of thing: Cash, SBI Current Account, Tuition Fees Income, Electricity Expense, Salary Payable, and so on. Every real-world event that has money impact is written into at least two notebooks so the story stays balanced. That is double-entry.

1.3 What clients usually expect from you

  • Explain which screen to open for a given transaction.
  • Explain whether a voucher is Receipt, Payment, Journal, or Contra.
  • Explain why debit and credit must always match.
  • Explain how fee collection appears in Cash/Bank Book and in income ledgers.
  • Explain Trial Balance, Profit & Loss (Surplus/Deficit), and Balance Sheet in plain language.
You do not need to become a Chartered Accountant. You need a clear mental model and enough worked examples to teach office staff without guessing.
Chapter 2

Golden rules of double-entry

Accounting has a vocabulary. Learn these words once; every screen in OneCampus uses them.

2.1 Assets, Liabilities, Income, Expenses

NatureMeaning in a collegeExamples in OneCampus
Asset What the college owns / is owed Cash, Bank, Computers, Furniture
Liability What the college owes others Salary Payable, PF Payable, Vendor Payable, TDS Payable
Income Money earned / received for services Tuition Fees, Admission Fees, Exam Fees, Donations
Expense Cost of running the college Salaries, Electricity, Stationery, Depreciation

2.2 Debit and Credit — stop fearing them

Debit (Dr) and Credit (Cr) are simply the left and right sides of an entry. They are not “good” and “bad”. The rule is mechanical:

Golden equation for every voucher:
Total Debits = Total Credits
If they are not equal, OneCampus will not allow posting.

Memory aid (college version)

If this happens…DebitCredit
Cash/Bank increases (money comes in) Cash or Bank Income / Receivable / Liability reduced
Cash/Bank decreases (money goes out) Expense / Asset / Payable reduced Cash or Bank
Expense incurred without paying cash yet Expense Payable (liability)
Cash deposited into bank Bank Cash

2.3 T-account picture

Every ledger account can be drawn as a “T”:

Debit (Dr)
Left side
Credit (Cr)
Right side

Example — Student pays ₹10,000 tuition by UPI into SBI

SBI Current A/c (Asset) — Debit
+ ₹10,000 (bank increases)
Tuition Fees (Income) — Credit
+ ₹10,000 (income increases)

2.4 Draft vs Posted

  • DRAFT — saved but not yet part of official books. Can be edited.
  • POSTED — locked into ledger postings. Appears in Cash Book, Bank Book, Trial Balance, P&L, Balance Sheet.
  • CANCELLED — voided; should not affect live balances (follow office policy for reversals).
Never tell a client “just type in the report”. Reports are the result of posted vouchers. If a report looks wrong, find the missing or wrong voucher.

Chapter 4

Accounts Dashboard

Menu: Finance & Accounts → Accounts Dashboard

The dashboard is your morning briefing. It does not replace books; it summarises them.

4.1 What you typically see

  • Cash balance and bank balance (from posted ledgers marked as cash/bank).
  • Count of draft vouchers waiting to be posted.
  • Recent postings.
  • Integration health: are Fees and Payroll auto-posting successfully?
  • Insights: YTD surplus/deficit signals, top income/expense heads, alerts.

4.2 How to explain it to a Principal

“This screen shows whether the college’s cash and bank positions look healthy, whether any vouchers are still unposted, and whether fee/payroll money is flowing into accounts automatically. Detailed investigation still happens in Cash Book, Bank Book, and Reports.”

4.3 Daily habit

  1. Open Dashboard after login.
  2. If drafts > 0, open Vouchers and clear them (post or correct).
  3. If fee/payroll integration shows failures, open Audit Trail / integration logs and Fee GL Mappings.
  4. Glance at cash vs bank — unusual drops need investigation.

5.3 When to add a new ledger

  • College opens a new bank account → create a new bank ledger (mark isBank).
  • New fee head that must appear separately in P&L → create income ledger + Fee GL mapping.
  • New recurring expense (e.g. Security Agency) → create expense ledger.
Do not create dozens of tiny ledgers without need. Too many ledgers confuse Trial Balance. Prefer meaningful heads that management actually reviews.

5.4 Client script

“Chart of Accounts is the list of buckets. Vouchers put money into buckets. Reports add up the buckets. If the bucket does not exist, we create it here first.”

Chapter 6

Financial Years

Menu: Financial Years (needs accounts:manage)

Indian educational institutions commonly follow the government financial year: 1 April to 31 March. Example: FY 2025–26 means 1 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2026.

6.1 Why FY matters

  • Voucher numbers restart / sequence per type per year (RV, PV, JV, …).
  • Reports are usually run for a date range inside an FY.
  • Budgets and depreciation are FY-aware.
  • Only an OPEN / active year should receive day-to-day postings.

6.2 Typical lifecycle

  1. Create next FY before 1 April.
  2. Complete year-end adjustments in old FY.
  3. Activate new FY for daily work.
  4. Lock or close old FY after audit (as per college policy).
Client tip: If staff cannot post a voucher, first check whether the correct financial year is active and the voucher date falls inside that year.
Chapter 7

Vouchers — the heart of accounting

Menu: Vouchers · Create at /admin/accounts/vouchers/new

A voucher is a formal document that records one accounting event. In OneCampus, almost everything important eventually becomes a voucher (manually or automatically).

7.1 Voucher types in OneCampus

CodeNamePrefixWhen to use
RECEIPTReceipt VoucherRVMoney coming into cash/bank
PAYMENTPayment VoucherPVMoney going out of cash/bank
JOURNALJournal VoucherJVAdjustments without immediate cash/bank movement
CONTRAContra VoucherCVTransfer between cash and bank (or bank↔bank)
OPENINGOpening BalanceOBStarting balances when books begin

7.2 Anatomy of a voucher

  • Voucher type & number (system-generated on post/create as configured)
  • Date
  • Narration (plain English story)
  • Lines: each line = one ledger + Debit or Credit + amount
  • Optional payment reference (cheque no., UTR, mode)
  • Status: DRAFT → POSTED

7.3 Posting rules enforced by the system

  1. At least two lines.
  2. Sum of debits = sum of credits.
  3. Ledgers must exist and belong to the tenant.
  4. Date must fit the financial year.
  5. On post: ledger balances update; immutable posting rows are written for books/reports.

7.4 Manual vs automatic vouchers

Manual Accountant opens Vouchers → New, chooses type, enters lines, posts.
Automatic Fees payment, payroll publish/paid, expense approval, depreciation run create vouchers via bridges.
Chapter 8

Receipt Voucher (money in)

Use a Receipt when cash or bank balance increases because of income or recovery.

8.1 Pattern

Debit: Cash or Bank (asset increases)
Credit: Income ledger (or liability/receivable as applicable)

Example A — Counter cash fee (manual illustration)

RV-2025-26-00041 · 12-Jun-2025 · Receipt · Narration: Tuition fee received from BA25-814 (cash)
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
CashDebit25,000.00
Tuition FeesCredit25,000.00

In live OneCampus, this often happens automatically when the Fees module records a CASH payment and Fee GL Mappings are configured (see Chapter 16).

Example B — UPI / gateway fee into SBI

RV-2025-26-00088 · 12-Jun-2025 · Receipt · UTR: 512345678901
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
SBI Current AccountDebit18,500.00
Tuition FeesCredit15,000.00
Examination FeesCredit3,500.00

One receipt can credit multiple income heads if the student paid a combined bill.

Example C — Alumni donation

RV-2025-26-00102 · 20-Jun-2025 · Donation for library development
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
SBI Current AccountDebit100,000.00
DonationsCredit100,000.00

Example D — Government grant

RV-2025-26-00140 · Grant received for lab equipment
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
SBI Current AccountDebit500,000.00
GrantsCredit500,000.00

8.2 Where it appears after posting

  • Cash Book (if Cash debited) or Bank Book (if bank debited)
  • General Ledger of each income head
  • Profit & Loss as income
  • Balance Sheet via higher cash/bank asset

Chapter 10

Journal Voucher (non-cash adjustments)

Journal vouchers record accounting truth when cash/bank does not move at that moment — or when we need to split an event into expense + liability.

Example A — Payroll accrual on publish (auto)

Assume June payroll: Gross ₹22,00,000; PF ₹1,80,000; PT ₹22,000; TDS ₹1,55,700; Net ₹18,42,300.

JV-2025-26-00030 · Payroll accrual June 2025
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
SalariesDebit22,00,000.00
PF PayableCredit1,80,000.00
PT PayableCredit22,000.00
TDS PayableCredit1,55,700.00
Salary PayableCredit18,42,300.00
Check: 22,00,000 debit = 1,80,000 + 22,000 + 1,55,700 + 18,42,300 credits. Balanced.

Example B — Monthly depreciation

JV-2025-26-00044 · Depreciation — Computers June 2025
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
DepreciationDebit25,000.00
Accumulated DepreciationCredit25,000.00

Example C — Correction: fee wrongly posted to Admission instead of Tuition

JV-2025-26-00051 · Reclassification of fee income
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
Admission FeesDebit5,000.00
Tuition FeesCredit5,000.00

No cash movement — only income heads are corrected.

Chapter 11

Contra Voucher (cash ↔ bank)

Contra is used when money moves between the college’s own cash and bank accounts. It is not income and not expense.

Deposit cash into bank → Debit Bank, Credit Cash
Withdraw cash from bank → Debit Cash, Credit Bank

Example A — Deposit day’s fee collection

CV-2025-26-00012 · Cash deposited in SBI
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
SBI Current AccountDebit1,25,000.00
CashCredit1,25,000.00

Example B — Withdraw cash for petty office use

CV-2025-26-00018 · Cash withdrawal
LedgerDr / CrAmount (₹)
CashDebit20,000.00
SBI Current AccountCredit20,000.00
Common client mistake: recording a cash deposit as a Receipt (income). That falsely inflates income. Always use Contra for own-fund transfers.

Chapter 16

Fee GL Mappings & auto-posting

Menu: Fee GL Mappings (Settings) · permission accounts:manage

This screen is the bridge between operational modules and accounts. If mappings are wrong, fee money may sit in Fees but not appear correctly in income ledgers.

16.1 Settings switches

  • autoPostFees — when ON, student fee payments create Receipt vouchers automatically.
  • autoPostPayroll — when ON, payroll publish creates Journal; payroll paid creates Payment.
  • Default cash / bank / income ledgers for fallbacks.

16.2 Fee head → income ledger

Fee head / source keyMaps to ledger
TUITION / SESSION-FEE / MONTHLY-FEETUITION-FEES
ADMISSIONADM-FEES
EXAMEXAM-FEES
HOSTELHOSTEL-FEES
LIBRARYLIBRARY-FEES

16.3 Payment mode → debit ledger

Payment modeDebit ledger
CASHCash
UPI / NEFT / RTGS / CHEQUE / GATEWAY / ONLINE / …Default bank (e.g. SBI-CA)

16.4 End-to-end fee story (teach this to clients)

  1. Student pays ₹15,000 tuition + ₹3,500 exam via UPI in Fees module.
  2. Fees records student receipt and clears dues.
  3. Bridge creates Receipt voucher: Dr SBI 18,500 / Cr Tuition 15,000 / Cr Exam 3,500.
  4. Bank Book and income ledgers update; P&L income rises.
Idempotency: the system logs each fee ledger entry so the same payment is not posted twice.

16.5 Payroll mapping

Component codes like PF, PT, TDS map to liability ledgers. Gross maps to Salaries expense; net to Salary Payable.

Chapter 20

Fixed Assets & depreciation

Menu: Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are long-life items: computers, furniture, projectors, buses, lab equipment. They are not fully expensed on purchase day (usually); their cost is spread over useful life as depreciation.

20.1 Asset register fields (conceptually)

  • Name, purchase date, cost, salvage value, useful life (months/years)
  • Method (OneCampus default: Straight Line)
  • Linked ledgers: Asset account, Accumulated Depreciation, Depreciation Expense

Example — Buy 20 computers for ₹12,00,000 on 01-May-2025

Payment voucher at purchase:

LedgerDr / CrAmount
FA-ComputersDebit12,00,000
SBI Current AccountCredit12,00,000

Suppose useful life 4 years, salvage ₹0. Annual depreciation = 12,00,000 / 4 = 3,00,000. Monthly ≈ 25,000. Running depreciation posts the Journal shown in Chapter 10.

20.2 Balance Sheet effect

Gross computers 12,00,000 minus Accumulated Depreciation (e.g. 25,000 after one month) gives net book value 11,75,000 shown under Fixed Assets.

Chapter 21

Bank Reconciliation

Menu: Bank Reconciliation

Every month the accountant compares the Bank Book with the bank’s PDF/CSV statement. Differences are normal for a few days; unresolved differences are dangerous.

21.1 Common differences

SituationIn Bank Book?In Bank Statement?Action
Cheque issued but not yet clearedYes (payment)Not yetTiming — wait / track
Fee UPI received, voucher missingNoYesCreate Receipt / fix Fees bridge
Bank chargesOften missingYesPayment/Journal for bank charges
Duplicate postingTwiceOnceCorrect via journal / cancel policy

21.2 OneCampus flow (conceptual)

  1. Create reconciliation for a bank ledger and period.
  2. Import / enter statement lines.
  3. Auto-match where amounts/dates align with postings.
  4. Manually match leftovers; investigate unmatched.
  5. Mark reconciled when book and statement agree after adjustments.
Never “force” reconciliation by ignoring unmatched bank credits. Unrecorded fee income is a classic fraud/control risk.

Three core reports every client meeting should mention:

22.2 Profit & Loss (Surplus / Deficit)

For non-profit colleges we often say Surplus (income > expense) or Deficit (expense > income) instead of “profit”.

Income side Tuition, Admission, Exam, Hostel, Grants, Donations…
Expense side Salaries, Electricity, Internet, Stationery, Depreciation…

Surplus / Deficit = Total Income − Total Expenses for the period.

22.3 Balance Sheet

Photograph of financial position on a date:

  • Assets — Cash, Bank, Fixed Assets (net of accum. dep.)
  • Liabilities — Payables, statutory dues
  • Capital / Accumulated Surplus — balancing figure including current surplus
Assets = Liabilities + Capital/Surplus
(Always — if not, a posting is missing or misclassified.)

22.4 Exports

Chapter 25

Client FAQ & common mistakes

Q1. Fees shows collection but Accounts income is low.

Check Fee GL Mappings, autoPostFees flag, payment-mode mapping, and integration logs. Unmapped heads may fall to a default or fail silently until investigated.

Q2. Can we edit a posted voucher?

Posted entries are meant to be permanent. Corrections are usually done with a reversing/adjusting Journal (office policy). Drafts can be edited freely.

Q3. Why is debit/credit confusing for office staff?

Teach patterns, not theory: “Money in → Debit bank, Credit income.” Practice with 10 receipts and 10 payments.

Q4. Is Contra an expense?

No. Contra only moves money between cash and bank.

Q5. Salary paid but Salaries expense looks double.

Expense is booked on payroll publish (Journal). Payment only clears Salary Payable. If someone also debits Salaries on payment, expense is doubled — wrong.

Q6. Trial Balance matches but Principal says reports are “wrong”.

Matching TB only proves arithmetic balance. Misclassification (donation posted as tuition) still balances. Review ledger-wise with management.

Common mistakes list

  • Using Receipt for cash deposit (should be Contra).
  • Posting personal student advances into income without policy.
  • Creating duplicate ledgers with similar names.
  • Leaving vouchers in DRAFT for weeks.
  • Ignoring bank charges on statement.
  • Not mapping new fee heads after fee structure changes.

  1. 3 students pay tuition ₹10,000 each by UPI (total ₹30,000).
  2. 1 student pays admission ₹5,000 cash.
  3. Pay stationery vendor ₹4,200 cash.
  4. Deposit ₹40,000 cash into SBI.
  5. Receive alumni donation ₹50,000 in SBI.
  6. Pay internet ₹8,900 by NEFT.

Closing positions (compute yourself, then check)
